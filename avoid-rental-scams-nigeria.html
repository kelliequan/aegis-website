7 Ways to Avoid Real Estate Scams When Renting an Apartment in Nigeria

If you have searched for an apartment in Lagos, Abuja or Port Harcourt, you have probably met at least one person who lost money to a fake listing. Rental scams are common because the process still runs on trust and speed, and scammers know that tenants are in a hurry. The good news is that most scams follow the same patterns, and you can spot them with a few simple checks.

Here are seven practical ways to protect yourself before you pay anything.

In this guide
  1. Check that the listing is real
  2. Confirm the agent represents the property
  3. Watch for several agents on one property
  4. Be careful with inspection and commitment fees
  5. Verify the agent before you pay
  6. Ask for the right documents
  7. Pay safely and keep records

1. Check that the listing is real

Fake listings are the most common trap. A scammer copies photos from a genuine listing or from the internet, posts them with a low price, and collects fees from people who never see the apartment.

  • Be suspicious of prices that look too good. A well-finished 3-bedroom flat in a prime area for the price of a self-contain is a warning sign.
  • Search the photos online. Use a reverse image search (Google Lens works well). If the same pictures appear under another address or another agent, walk away.
  • Insist on seeing it in person. A video call can help you shortlist, but only a physical visit proves the apartment exists and is available.
  • Check that the details match. The address, number of rooms and condition on the day should match the advert. Gaps are a red flag.

2. Confirm the agent represents the property

Some people advertise apartments they have no right to rent out. They may know the landlord, have seen a vacant flat, or simply be using another agent's listing without permission.

  • Ask who the landlord or owner is and how the agent was appointed.
  • Ask for written authority. A genuine agent can show a letter, a signed instruction or a management agreement from the owner or the property manager.
  • Speak to the landlord or the caretaker yourself where possible, and confirm that this agent is the one handling the property.
  • Do not rely on the gateman alone. A caretaker can confirm the flat is vacant, but not that the person asking you for money is allowed to collect it.

3. Watch for several agents on one property

It is normal for a property to be shared with other agents, and that alone is not a scam. The risk starts when agents who do not actually hold the mandate quote different prices, or when two people collect money for the same flat.

  • Ask which agent holds the mandate and who is working with them.
  • Compare the price you are given with what the landlord or caretaker says. Big differences mean someone is adding their own margin or has no authority.
  • Pay only one party, once, and only after you know who is entitled to receive the money.
  • Be wary of "another client is paying today" pressure. Real demand exists, but a rush is also how people get hurried past these checks.

4. Be careful with inspection and commitment fees

Many agents charge a modest fee to cover transport and time on inspection day, and that is not automatically a scam. The danger is paying a fee before you have verified anything, or paying large "commitment" or "reservation" amounts to hold a flat you have not seen.

  • Never pay any fee for an apartment you have not seen. If an agent asks for money to "send the address" or "hold it for you", stop.
  • Keep inspection fees small and ask what the fee covers. Agree the amount before you go.
  • Ask what happens to a commitment fee. Is it refundable? Is it part of the rent? Get the answer in writing before you pay.
  • Remember that fees can be negotiated. Agency and legal fees are normally paid when you take the apartment, not before you have seen it. Check the current rules for your state, as tenancy laws differ, for example in Lagos.

5. Verify the agent before you pay

Treat an agent like anyone you are about to trust with a large sum of money. A few minutes of checking can save you your rent.

  • Ask for a government ID and confirm the name matches the name on the payment account.
  • Visit the office if the agent has one, or at least confirm the business address exists.
  • Check the company. A registered business can be searched on the Corporation Affairs Commission (CAC) public search. Estate surveyors and valuers are registered with ESVARBON, and in Lagos you can also check the state's real estate regulator.
  • Look at their track record. Ask for references from recent tenants and call them. Search the agent's name and phone number online for complaints.
  • Trust consistency. A real agent has the same name, number and account details on the advert, on WhatsApp and on the receipt.

6. Ask for the right documents before you pay

Documents will not make a deal perfect, but a seller or agent who cannot produce any of them is telling you something. Before paying, ask to see:

  • Proof of ownership or authority. This could be the landlord's title document (such as a Certificate of Occupancy, deed of assignment or survey plan), or a signed letter of authority from the owner to the agent.
  • The landlord's ID, and a recent utility bill or receipt that links the person to the property.
  • A draft tenancy agreement. Read it before you pay. It should name the correct landlord, the property address, the rent, the period and the rules you are agreeing to.
  • A breakdown of all charges. Rent, agency fee, legal fee, caution deposit and service charge should each be listed, so nothing is added later.
  • Receipts for everything you pay, issued in the name of the person or company you paid.

If you are not sure how to read a document, ask a lawyer or a trusted person to look at it before you pay. It costs far less than losing your rent.

7. Pay safely and keep records

The way you pay matters as much as who you pay. Once money leaves your account, it is very hard to recover.

  • Pay by bank transfer, not cash. A transfer leaves a record. Cash leaves you with nothing to show.
  • Check the account name. It should match the landlord or the registered company. Be careful when asked to pay a personal account with a different name.
  • Sign the agreement before you hand over the full rent, and get your receipt immediately.
  • Pay in stages where you can. A small amount to reserve a flat you have inspected, and the balance when you collect the keys, is safer than paying everything on day one.
  • Keep screenshots and messages. Save chats, adverts, receipts and call details. They will matter if there is a dispute.
  • Do not be rushed. If you feel pressure to pay before the checks above, that pressure is itself a warning sign.
Quick checklist before you pay
  • I have seen the apartment in person.
  • The photos are not used elsewhere online.
  • I have confirmed the agent's authority with the landlord or in writing.
  • The agent's identity and business have been checked.
  • I have seen the documents and read the agreement.
  • The account name matches the landlord or company.
  • I will get a receipt and keep every record.

If you think you have been scammed

Act quickly. Report to your bank straight away, keep every message and receipt, and report to the police. You can also report to the state's real estate regulator or the agent's professional body if they belong to one. The sooner you report, the better the chance of tracing the money.
